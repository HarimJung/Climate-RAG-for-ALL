# Visual Climate DESIGN.md

## Product Intent
Visual Climate turns complex public climate data into understandable, trustworthy, and actionable country intelligence.

## Core Feel
The product should feel:
- premium
- precise
- calm
- editorial
- trustworthy
- globally relevant
- data-rich but never cluttered

## Design Direction
Blend:
- structured SaaS clarity
- editorial readability
- public-data credibility
- modern climate intelligence aesthetics

Avoid:
- generic AI startup look
- excessive gradients
- noisy dashboards
- overdesigned NGO-style visuals
- heavy visual drama

## Primary User Outcomes
Users should understand within one screen:
1. what Visual Climate does
2. why it matters
3. what they can do next
4. why the data is trustworthy

## Primary Audiences
- teachers and students
- journalists and researchers
- policy / NGO teams
- sustainability and disclosure teams

## Messaging Hierarchy
1. Climate accountability for every country
2. Public data translated into understandable climate intelligence
3. Grades, classifications, and transparent methodology
4. Shareable posters and reporting usefulness
5. Trusted sources and explainable scoring

## Visual Tone
- bright but not sterile
- elegant but not flashy
- analytical but not cold
- clear before impressive

## Color Rules
- neutral light background
- strong semantic colors only when meaning matters
- green = strong progress
- amber = partial progress
- red/orange = weak performance
- blue/gray = methodology or supporting information

## Typography
- strong headlines
- clean sans-serif
- highly legible numbers
- readable body copy
- short paragraphs
- explanation-first language

## Card System
Prioritize:
- grade card
- domain score cards
- trust/source cards
- insight summary cards
- poster preview cards
- guide teaser cards

## Chart Principles
- explanatory, not decorative
- always support with short interpretation
- highlight key trends and comparisons
- do not overload legends or labels
- charts must be export-friendly

## Copy Style
Write like:
- a clear analyst
- a public educator
- a calm editor

Do:
- explain what the user is seeing
- use plain language
- say why it matters

Do not:
- use hype-heavy AI language
- assume expert knowledge
- rely on labels without interpretation

## Implementation Guardrails
- preserve backend contracts
- preserve existing routes
- preserve existing skills and agents
- preserve scoring logic
- preserve data pipeline behavior
- redesign presentation layer first
